## What the best work has in common (the rules we adopt)

1. **One committed material per section.** Dither, clay, fire, watercolour, risograph. Never a generic
   "clean 3D render". The material is the idea.
2. **A persistent device** carries the cut: a patch (Moncler), a polaroid (Rimowa), a card frame
   (Čmára), a coin (Zack Lee), a picture frame (Kapi). We use a circle that changes material.
3. **Two speeds, nothing in between.** Long holds of 10–25 s (Soft Logic, clay, Burberry) against
   stroboscopic runs at 0.08–0.2 s (Rimowa, Monsters, NOMINT, Mac Miller). Stillness is a tool.
4. **Hard colour commitment.** One or two hues per shot (Tinariwen, OpenTable, Riso duotone).
   Unintuitive palettes (magenta desert) beat correct ones.
5. **Type is sparse, exact and placed in the world.** One sentence on a flat card; small tracked caps;
   condensed display caps; pull quotes; marquee repetition. Never a lower third, never glow.
6. **Texture passes at the reference's intensity.** Grain, paper, halftone, dither, chromatic
   aberration, scanlines, light leaks, torn edges. Applied per section, not globally.
7. **Process is content.** Hands, desks, breakdown layers, scanned paper. The film can show its making.
8. **Format play inside the frame.** 9:16 slivers, cards, split frames, zoom-out reveals.
9. **Something new every 2–4 s**, and a true reveal at the end (Dealies' hands, Hermès' insignia).

## What this means for Higgsfield

Models paint materials well: ink in water, clay with fingerprints, burning steel wool, watercolour
bleeds, thermal footage, painted skies. They cannot do rules 3, 5, 6, 8 or 9: those are editing and
code. So the system in docs/system.md generates *plates* of material and finishes everything else in
`window.seek(t)`. See docs/matter_shotlist.md for the film built on these rules.
